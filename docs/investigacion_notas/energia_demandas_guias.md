## 1. Energy expenditure of football/futsal players (match, training, rest days)

### Takeaway
Doubly-labelled-water (DLW) data exist only for professional *football* players (about 3,000–3,600 kcal/day in men, about 2,900 kcal/day in professional women). They show that **energy expenditure varies much less between days than carbohydrate needs do**. No DLW or accelerometry-validated energy expenditure data were found for futsal players, amateur or elite. Futsal-specific "energy" studies are small Brazilian intake surveys that use estimated requirements.

### Cited Findings
- Anderson L, Orme P, Naughton RJ, Close GL, Milsom J, Rydings D, O'Boyle A, Di Michele R, Louis J, Hambly C, Speakman JR, Morgans R, Drust B, Morton JP. "Energy Intake and Expenditure of Professional Soccer Players of the English Premier League: Evidence of Carbohydrate Periodization." *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 2017;27(3):128–138 (DOI 10.1123/ijsnem.2016-0259 [K: DOI from memory; journal, volume and pages V]). Study design: n=6 EPL players, DLW over 7 days in season (5 training days, 2 match days). Mean TDEE was **3,566 ± 585 kcal/day** and mean intake 3,186 kcal/day, not significantly different. Intake was **3,789 kcal on match days vs 2,956 kcal on training days**. Carbohydrate intake was **about 6.4 g/kg on match days vs about 4.2 g/kg on training days**. Protein and fat were not periodized. Elite only; very small n. — [LJMU repository](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/6323/); [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/energy-intake-and-expenditure-of-professional-soccer-players-of-t); [mysportscience summary](https://www.mysportscience.com/post/2017/01/09/intake-of-english-premier-league-soccer-players) [V for the figures via abstract and summaries]
- A later commentary on Anderson 2017 noted that carbohydrate intake on the day before a match and during recovery did not reach the guideline amounts for glycogen storage. — [Huddersfield record](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/energy-intake-and-expenditure-of-professional-soccer-players-of-t/) [S]
- Case study of a Premier League goalkeeper (Anderson et al. 2019, *IJSPP*), using DLW: **2,894 kcal/day**, against about 3,500 kcal/day for outfield teammates in the same microcycle. Position matters. — [LJMU PDF](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/9865/3/Goalkeeper_Case_Study_IJSPP_V6.pdf) [S]
- Pooled DLW range for professional male players: **about 12.5–14.6 MJ/day (about 3,000–3,500 kcal/day)**, citing Anderson 2016/2017 and Brinkmans et al. 2019. A recent DLW pre-season study (14 days; training-only vs training-plus-match microcycles) found a mean TDEE of **about 13.25 MJ/day (about 3,170 kcal)** and **no significant difference in TDEE between training-only and training-plus-match microcycles**. Players did not match intake to daily demands. — [PMC12948648](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12948648) [S: snippet only. Full text was blocked, so authors, year and DOI were not captured.]
- Professional female players (Dutch league), DLW over 14 days: **2,882 ± 278 kcal/day**. — [Maastricht record](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/energy-expenditure-and-dietary-intake-in-professional-female-foot) [S]
- Futsal-specific: a Brazilian cross-sectional study (n=10 adult "indoor soccer" athletes, *Rev Bras Nutr Esportiva* 2015) reported an **estimated** TEE of about 4,110 kcal/day, with intake covering about 55.5% of it. The method was an estimate, not DLW. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/6ca4b709f45440a989258a6531626d5d) [S]
- Other futsal studies show low-quality intake data with energy and carbohydrate deficits. Adolescents: deficit of about 396 kcal/day (2016). Boys aged 10–13: estimated need 3,051 vs intake 1,827 kcal/day (2017). Adults: 76.9% of 13 athletes were below the recommended energy intake (2018). — [DOAJ 2016](https://doaj.org/article/3ce56effbaa043919c521d2c107d269d), [DOAJ 2017](https://doaj.org/article/9e36286256814d1e83e7e08f3904e12b), [DOAJ 2018](https://doaj.org/article/a974b0288f8b474d9b5a0116ef3f1e2c) [S]
- Brazilian national futsal team: energy deficit and carbohydrate intake of about **3.6 g/kg/day**, below guidelines. Azorean senior futsal players met the protein recommendations but not the energy or carbohydrate ones. — [ResearchGate: Brazilian national team](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355207262_Nutritional_Knowledge_and_Eating_Habits_of_the_National_Brazilian_Futsal_Team); [PMC10055704: Azorean senior futsal](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10055704/) [S]
- Systematic review of indoor team sports (*Nutrients* 2022;14(22):4755): most athletes did not meet recommendations, and **85.7% were below the 6 g/kg carbohydrate lower bound**. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/22/4755) [S]

### Inferences
- In football, total daily expenditure changes only modestly between match, training and rest days. What changes a lot is the **carbohydrate need around the session**. This supports expressing day-type targets mainly as carbohydrate g/kg rather than as kcal.
- A typical amateur futsal player (2–3 sessions plus 1 match a week, matches of 40 min effective time with rolling substitutions) almost certainly spends less than an EPL player. The DLW figures should be treated as an upper reference, not a target.
- Underfuelling, especially of carbohydrate, is the consistent finding in futsal. A risk worth flagging in the app is eating too little, not eating too much.

### Gaps
- No DLW, indirect-calorimetry or validated-accelerometry TDEE data for futsal players at any level were found.
- No DLW data for amateur football players were found. Rest-day TDEE has not been isolated in the DLW studies found.
- A Ukrainian study of 60 professional futsal players, energy expenditure by position, was found as an abstract with no figures.

## 2. Practical estimation methods (RMR equations × activity factor) and g/kg vs kcal

### Takeaway
In athletes, the FFM-based Cunningham equation and the ten Haaf (2014) weight-based equation predict resting energy expenditure within ±10% for roughly 75–85% of people. Harris-Benedict, Mifflin-St Jeor and similar equations do so for fewer than 50%. Multiplying by an activity factor adds further error. Guidelines (ACSM/AND/DC 2016, UEFA 2021, SEÑ 2025) express carbohydrate and protein targets in **g/kg body mass per day, scaled to load**, rather than as a calorie number.

### Cited Findings
- ten Haaf T, Weijs PJM. "Resting energy expenditure prediction in recreational athletes of 18–35 years: confirmation of Cunningham equation and an improved weight-based alternative." *PLoS One* 2014;9(10):e108460. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0108460, PMID 25275434. Sample: n=90 (53 men, 37 women), aged 23.2 ± 4.8 years, training 9.1 ± 5.0 h/week (minimum 3 h and 2 sessions a week). These are **recreational athletes**, so the sample is relevant to amateurs. Share of predictions within ±10% of measured REE:
  - Cunningham: **84.9% (men) / 78.4% (women)**
  - new weight-based equation: **83.0% / 75.7%**
  - new FFM-based equation: **83.0% / 72.3%**
  - De Lorenzo: 77.4% / 59.5%
  - Harris-Benedict, Mifflin, Owen, FAO/WHO and Schofield: all **less than 50%**

  Cunningham as given in the paper is **REE = 22 × FFM + 500 kcal/day**. Limitations: the new equations were not cross-validated (developed and tested on the same data), the sample was Dutch only, and all equations underestimated higher REE values. — [PMC4183531](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4183531) [V]
- Later evidence is mixed. In master athletes, De Lorenzo was most accurate, closely followed by Cunningham (Frings-Meuthen et al. 2021, *Front Physiol*, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2021.641455). A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis is reported to have found ten Haaf (age, weight, height) most accurate, though "not massively" different from other equations. — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2021.641455/full); [JISSN 2021 related paper](https://Jissn.Biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12970-021-00465-x.pdf); [Biolayne summary of the 2023 meta-analysis](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-17/are-resting-metabolic-rate-equations-accurate/) [S: the 2023 meta-analysis was not identified by author or DOI]
- Practical point: Cunningham needs FFM, which an app user will not know. ten Haaf's weight-based equation and Mifflin-St Jeor need only weight, height, age and sex. The ten Haaf coefficients were in images that could not be read this session and must be copied from the paper. [inference plus V]
- ACSM/AND/DC 2016 joint position: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. "American College of Sports Medicine Joint Position Statement. Nutrition and Athletic Performance." *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2016;48(3):543–568 (DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852). Also published as the AND position in *J Acad Nutr Diet* 2016;116(3):501–528 (DOI 10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006). It expresses carbohydrate targets in g/kg/day by load:
  - light (low intensity or skill-based): **3–5 g/kg**
  - moderate (about 1 h/day): **5–7 g/kg**
  - high (1–3 h/day of moderate to high intensity): **6–10 g/kg**
  - very high (more than 4–5 h/day): **8–12 g/kg**

  Protein: **1.2–2.0 g/kg/day**. Fat: usually 20–35% of energy and not below about 20%. It warns about low energy availability rather than prescribing a kcal target. [K: widely reproduced figures, but not re-fetched this session. Check against the PDF.]
- The 2022 indoor-team-sports review states the team-sport carbohydrate target as **6–10 g/kg/day, up to about 12 g/kg on pre-season days or very intense sessions over 4 h**. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/22/4755) [S]
- IOC consensus on REDs (Mountjoy M et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2023;57(17):1073–1097, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994). Energy availability is expressed in kcal/kg FFM per day. A sustained value **below about 30 kcal/kg FFM/day** is the commonly cited problematic level, though the 2023 statement stresses that no single universal threshold exists. EA requires knowing FFM and exercise energy expenditure, which an app cannot measure. [K: not re-fetched this session]

### Inferences
- For a v0.1 app, **g/kg targets per day type do not require estimating kcal at all**. Carbohydrate (g/kg by day type) and protein (g/kg, constant) are computed from body weight alone, which the app already asks for. An optional "approximate kcal" display could use ten Haaf weight-based REE × a PAL factor, labelled as a rough estimate with about ±10% error before the activity factor is applied.
- Showing a calorie number invites restriction behaviour, which the project's safety rules forbid. Using g/kg also avoids that.

### Gaps
- No validated activity factors (PAL) specific to futsal were found. Generic PAL tables are not sport-validated.
- The exact ten Haaf weight-based coefficients were not captured, and neither was the identity of the 2023 meta-analysis.

## 3. Futsal match demands and justification for day-type nutrition

### Takeaway
Futsal is a high-intensity intermittent sport. Over a match of 2 × 20 min of stopped-clock time (75–90 min of real time, with unlimited rolling substitutions), outfield players cover about 4 km at about 100–120 m/min. Mean heart rate is about 85–90% of HRmax and mean VO2 about 75–80% of VO2max, with blood lactate around 4–5.5 mmol/L and frequent repeated sprints. Neuromuscular fatigue and biochemical disturbance last up to 24 h. Together, this justifies carbohydrate loading on match days and the day before, plus recovery nutrition, which are glycogen-dependent demands. Most of the data come from elite or sub-elite players, and little GPS/accelerometry data from official games exist.

### Cited Findings
Spyrou K, Freitas TT, Marín-Cascales E, Alcaraz PE. "Physical and Physiological Match-Play Demands and Player Characteristics in Futsal: A Systematic Review." *Front Psychol* 2020;11:569897. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897. PRISMA review; searches up to 25 July 2020; **61 studies included**. — [Frontiers full text](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full) [V]

**Match format**
- Two 20-min halves with a stopped clock and a 10-min break; real duration 75–90 min.
- Unlimited substitutions; squad of up to 14 (9 substitutes); one 1-min timeout per half.
- **Effective playing time per player was not reported** in the review.

**Total distance per match**
- Barbero-Álvarez 2008: 4,313 ± 2,139 m
- Dogramaci 2011 (elite): 4,277 ± 1,030 m
- Ribeiro 2020 (GPS): 3,749 ± 1,123 m
- Milanez 2020: 3,046 ± 1,485 m
- Makaje 2012: outfield 5,087 ± 1,104 m; goalkeepers 2,043 ± 702 m
- The review's summary: "around 4 km"

**Distance per minute**
- Barbero-Álvarez 2008: 117.3 ± 11.6 m/min
- Milioni 2016: 103.2 (1st half) and 96.4 (2nd half) m/min
- Bueno 2014: 97.9 and 90.3 m/min

**High intensity and sprints**
- Castagna 2009: high-intensity running (≥15.5 km/h) took 12% of match time and sprinting (≥18.3 km/h) 5%. One sprint every 79 s, averaging 10.5 m and 1.95 s, with less than 40 s of recovery.
- Caetano 2015: 26 ± 13 sprints per match, often in pairs about 15 s apart.
- Milioni 2016: about 49 sprints in the 1st half and 45 in the 2nd.
- Ribeiro 2020: 87 ± 49 high-intensity accelerations and 80 ± 32 decelerations.

**Heart rate**
- Barbero-Álvarez 2008: 174 ± 7 bpm (90 ± 2% HRmax), with **83% of time at ≥85% HRmax**
- Other studies: 83–93% HRmax (Rodrigues 2011 86.4%; Yiannaki 2020 87.7%; Charlot 2016 83.2%; Bekris 2020 93%)
- Makaje 2012: outfield 89.8% vs goalkeepers 73.7%

**VO2**
- Castagna 2009: 48.6 ml/kg/min (76% of VO2max), with 46% of time above 80% of VO2max
- Rodrigues 2011: 79.2% of VO2max

**Blood lactate**
- Castagna 2009: 5.3 mmol/L
- Milioni 2016: 4.8 and 4.2 mmol/L by half
- Makaje 2012: 5.5 mmol/L
- Bekris 2020: about 15 mmol/L, sampled at substitution, so not comparable with the others

**Recovery**
- Decreases in peak force and voluntary activation, plus raised CK and a lower testosterone/cortisol ratio, lasting **up to 24 h** after the match.

**Elite vs sub-elite**
- Elite players covered 42% more distance (4,277 vs 3,011 m; Dogramaci 2011) and performed more high-intensity actions.
- VO2max: elite 62.9 ± 5.3 vs sub-elite 55.2 ± 5.7 ml/kg/min (Barbero-Álvarez 2009).
- Elite anthropometrics: about 70 kg, 1.76 m and 15% body fat.

**Gaps the authors note**
- Only 2 studies used wearables in official games.
- Protocols were heterogeneous.
- There are no energy expenditure figures.
- Energy-system contributions were not quantified.

Naser N, Ali A, Macadam P. "Physical and physiological demands of futsal." *J Exerc Sci Fit* 2017;15(2):76–80. DOI 10.1016/j.jesf.2017.09.001. Open-access **narrative** review covering match analysis, physiology, energy requirements, fitness testing and skills. It concludes that fatigue builds as the match goes on because of high intensity and repeated maximal sprints, and that players need intermittent endurance, repeated-sprint ability and leg power. — [AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/b954c272-9013-4e85-ba13-ada84f5a344a/full); [PMC5812879](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5812879) [V for the bibliographic data; content from the abstract]

FIFA Futsal Intermittent Endurance Test (FIET) distances (Naser & Ali 2016, as cited in Spyrou 2020): elite 1,378 m, sub-elite 1,018 m, **social/recreational 781 m**. This is one of the few data points on recreational players. — [Spyrou 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full) [V]

A 2025 *Frontiers in Physiology* futsal paper appeared in the search (DOI 10.3389/fphys.2025.1642594) but was not read. — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1642594/pdf) [unread]

### Inferences
- Futsal intensity, measured by HR, %VO2max and lactate, is comparable to or higher than football per minute played. Distance and duration are smaller, and rolling substitutions spread the load. Per-day energy needs of amateurs are therefore probably moderate, while match-day carbohydrate availability (glycogen for repeated sprints) is what matters most.
- Day-type logic follows from this:
  - **match day and the day before**: higher carbohydrate (glycogen-dependent repeated sprints)
  - **day after the match**: recovery carbohydrate and protein (fatigue up to 24 h)
  - **hard training**: moderate to high carbohydrate
  - **light training or rest**: lower carbohydrate
  - protein stays roughly constant
- Amateur players play fewer minutes and at lower intensity than elite ones (elite players cover about 42% more distance). Day-type targets for amateurs should sit at the lower end of the ranges.

### Gaps
- No data on effective minutes played per player (rotation patterns) were found in the reviews.
- No quantified aerobic/anaerobic energy contribution for futsal.
- Very little data on amateur or recreational futsal match demands (only the FIET "social" level).
- No systematic review of futsal match demands published after 2020 was confirmed this session.

## 4. Status of the main sports-nutrition guidelines in 2026, and evidence gaps

### Takeaway
As of November 2026, the **ACSM/AND/DC 2016 joint position "Nutrition and Athletic Performance" is still the current version, but an update is formally under way**. ACSM's board approved it in Q1 2026, and the writing group was recruited with a deadline of 26 June 2026. No publication date has been announced. **UEFA 2021 (Collins et al.)** remains the most recent football-specific expert statement. FIFA's own guide dates from the 2005/2006 F-MARC consensus. No futsal-specific nutrition guideline or consensus exists, including from RFEF, AEMEF or the Consejo General de Dietistas-Nutricionistas. The most relevant new Spanish document is the **Spanish Nutrition Society (SEÑ) consensus, *Nutrients* 2025**.

### Inferences
- Recommended citation set for the app's rules, with each rule marked "pending validation by a dietitian-nutritionist":
  1. ACSM/AND/DC 2016 (Thomas et al.) for carbohydrate g/kg by load and protein g/kg. It is the general baseline, and its update is under way.
  2. UEFA 2021 (Collins et al.) for the football-specific match-day and day-before amounts (6–8 g/kg), the pre-match meal (1–3 g/kg, 3–4 h before) and in-match carbohydrate (30–60 g/h). It is elite-focused, so amateurs should use the lower end.
  3. SEÑ 2025 as a recent Spanish consensus that explicitly includes recreational athletes.
  4. IOC REDs 2023 as the safety reference against underfuelling.
- Because the ACSM update is under way, the code should store the guideline version and year next to each rule, so the rules can be updated when the new position appears.
- The app does not need to estimate calories: g/kg targets by day type cover everything the guidelines prescribe. If it shows kcal at all, the number should be clearly labelled as an approximate reference.

### Gaps
- Evidence is futsal-specific only for match demands (around 61 studies, mostly elite or sub-elite, few using wearables). For energy expenditure and nutrient needs, essentially all guidance is **extrapolated from football and generic team-sport or endurance research**. There are no futsal nutrition intervention trials and no futsal guideline.
- Amateur-specific evidence is very thin: ten Haaf 2014 (recreational athletes, REE only), the FIET "social" level, and small Brazilian intake surveys.
- The status of the ACSM update after June 2026 is unknown: no draft or publication date was found.
- The AEMEF, Consejo General de Dietistas-Nutricionistas and RFEF futsal websites were not searched directly. They can only be described as "not found in searches", not as confirmed to have no position.
- The exact figures in SEÑ 2025, ACSM 2016 and UEFA 2021 should be checked against the full texts before they go into code. Automated access to PMC and PubMed was blocked this session.
