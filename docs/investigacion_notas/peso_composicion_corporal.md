# "Optimal body weight" and body composition in futsal players, and the safety limits for an app that talks about weight

User context: healthy adult amateur winger (ala), about 1.72 m, 63 kg, BMI about 21.3 (63 / 1.72² = 21.3). Project rule: the app must never set weight-loss goals or restrict calories.

## 1. Does the literature support an "ideal weight" for an athlete, or does it focus on body composition, performance and health?

### Takeaway
No. All three major statements explicitly reject a single "ideal" body mass or body-fat value. They talk about individualized ranges, changes over the season, performance and health, and they want assessment done by trained professionals. High consensus (joint position statements and expert consensus).

### Cited Findings
- **ACSM/AND/DC 2016** (Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. *Nutrition and Athletic Performance*. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543–568; DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852; also J Acad Nutr Diet 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006):
  - "athletic performance cannot be accurately predicted solely based on body weight and composition. A single and rigid 'optimal' body composition should not be recommended for any event or group of athletes." — [Thomas et al. 2016, ResearchGate MSSE version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/297695609_Nutrition_and_Athletic_Performance); [Dietitians of Canada summary](https://www.dietitians.ca/DietitiansOfCanada/media/Documents/Resources/noap-position-paper.pdf)
  - Key point 5: body composition for optimal performance "needs to be individualized and periodized. Care should be taken to preserve health and long term performance by avoiding practices that create unacceptably low energy availability and psychological stress." — [Thomas et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/297695609_Nutrition_and_Athletic_Performance)
  - "There are technical errors associated with all body composition techniques that limit the usefulness of measurement for athlete selection and performance prediction. In lieu of setting absolute body composition goals … it is preferred that normative data are provided in terms of ranges." Goals "should be set in terms of ranges that can be appropriately tracked." — [Thomas et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/297695609_Nutrition_and_Athletic_Performance)
  - "body mass index is not appropriate as a body composition surrogate in athletes." The statement also warns that athletes may feel pressure to reach "unrealistically low targets of weight/body fat", which exposes them to "chronic periods of low EA" and disordered eating. — [Dietitians of Canada summary of the 2016 paper](https://www.dietitians.ca/DietitiansOfCanada/media/Documents/Resources/noap-position-paper.pdf)
  - Context: the earlier 2000 version of the same statement was more explicit. It gave sport-level %fat ranges (6–19% for male "power sports such as football"), an "estimated minimal level of body fat compatible with health" of 5% for males and 12% for females, and "it is inappropriate to set a specific body-fat percentage goal for an individual athlete" because of a 3–4% error with skinfolds or BIA. These figures come from the **2000** statement (MSSE 32(12):2130–2145), which the 2016 paper superseded, so treat them as historical. — [ACSM/ADA/DC 2000](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2000/12000/nutrition_and_athletic_performance.25.aspx)
- **UEFA 2021** (Collins J, Maughan RJ, Gleeson M, et al. *UEFA expert group statement on nutrition in elite football*. Br J Sports Med 2021;55(8):416–442; DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2019-101961; online October 2020). This is a narrative expert synthesis, not a systematic review.
  - "The optimum physique … varies according to an individual player's physiology, and their field position and playing style. Indeed, there is no single value for either BM or FM content against which targets or judgements should be made." — [Collins et al. 2021, BJSM full text](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/bjsports/55/8/416.full.pdf)
  - "Mean FM levels in elite male players measured by DXA typically range from ~8% to 13%, although lower and higher values have also been recorded." Goalkeepers are "typically taller and heavier with greater FM than outfield players." These are elite **football (soccer)** figures, not futsal. — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/8/416)
  - "both male and female players may perform well with FM levels outside the normally accepted range: it is not known if a change in physique would result in better performance." — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/8/416)
  - "Each player's body composition goals should be agreed between the player and the performance team." Players may want a lean or muscular look, and "the desire for a lean or muscular physique may conflict with the player's performance goals." — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/bjsports/55/8/416.full.pdf)
  - UEFA's press summary: "There is no single body mass or body fat percentage value that fits all players." — [UEFA.com 2020](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/0262-10b3a43fd486-f4484edc6615-1000--uefa-launches-expert-group-statement-on-nutrition-for-elit/)
- **IOC REDs 2023** (Mountjoy M, Ackerman KE, Bailey DM, et al. *2023 IOC consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)*. Br J Sports Med 2023;57(17):1073–1097; DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994):
  - Body composition work is "important for optimising health and athletic performance", but pressure to reach an "athletic look" can lead "to body dissatisfaction and LEA, and then to symptoms of REDs, DE behaviours or EDs." — [Mountjoy et al. 2023 (PDF)](https://forening.sls.se/media/a4cl4crj/idrottsnutrition_2023-ioc-consenses-on-red-s-1.pdf)
  - Body composition decisions should be "health first–performance second". — same source

### Inferences
- The question "what is my optimal weight?" has no single number as an answer in the evidence. What the guidance supports is: (a) a health-first, individualized range; (b) judging it by performance and well-being; (c) measurement by qualified professionals.
- BMI 21.3 sits in the general-population "normal" band. But ACSM/AND/DC 2016 says BMI is not a valid body-composition surrogate in athletes, so the app should not use BMI to judge an athlete.

### Gaps
- I found no futsal-specific position statement on body composition. Futsal guidance borrows from football (UEFA) and general sports nutrition (ACSM/AND/DC, IOC).
- I could not access the full IOC 2018/2023 text on non-REDs body composition guidance beyond the section quoted.

## 2. Anthropometric and body composition data for futsal players: level, position, method

### Takeaway
Elite male futsal players average about 70 kg, about 1.76 m and about 15% body fat. That 15% is a pooled figure from mixed methods. With DXA, values range from about 7% to about 16% depending on the sample. Wingers tend to be the lightest and leanest outfield players, and goalkeepers and pivots the heaviest. Several studies find **no clear difference in body composition between elite and lower-level players**. Data on amateur wingers are very scarce, and figures from different methods cannot be compared directly.

### Cited Findings
**Systematic reviews**
- Spyrou K, Freitas TT, Marín-Cascales E, Alcaraz PE. *Physical and Physiological Match-Play Demands and Player Characteristics in Futsal: A Systematic Review.* Front Psychol 2020;11:569897. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897 (UCAM, Murcia).
  - Elite players: "weigh, on average, ~70 kg, … measure ~1.76 m of height and … display ~15% of body fat" (citing Jovanovic 2011; Garrido-Chamorro 2012). — [Spyrou et al. 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full)
  - Values by position, from de Moura et al. 2013 as cited by Spyrou (the review prints the height units and SDs inconsistently, so treat with caution):

    | Position | Height | Body mass | Body fat |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Goalkeeper | ~1.78 m | 74 ± 2.5 kg | 13 ± 2% |
    | Defender (cierre) | ~1.74 m | 69 ± 2 kg | 10 ± 2% |
    | **Winger (ala)** | **~1.69 m** | **68 ± 2 kg** | **11 ± 2%** |
    | Pivot | ~1.73 m | 71 ± 2 kg | 10 ± 2% |

    — [Spyrou et al. 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full)
  - Ramos-Campo et al. 2014 (cited by Spyrou) ranked body fat from highest to lowest: pivots > goalkeepers > backs > wingers. Baroni & Leal Junior 2010 found 22 goalkeepers heavier and taller than 164 court players. — [Spyrou et al. 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full)
  - Comparisons by level: López-Fernández et al. 2020 found similar fat mass in elite and sub-elite players, with more leg lean mass in elite players. Pedro et al. 2013 found no meaningful anthropometric differences between professional and semi-professional players. Spyrou concludes that players "present low percentage of body fat, which seems commonplace among athletes from different on-court positions and competition levels." — [Spyrou et al. 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full); [search summary](https://www.doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897)
  - Spyrou does not report the measurement method of each included study, which is a limitation of the pooled "~15%" figure. — [Spyrou et al. 2020](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897/full)
- A 2020 systematic review of elite male futsal anthropometry and fitness (Movement & Sport Sciences) included 10 observational studies with PEDro scores of 4–7. It found that profiles differ by position and called for more research "to define an appropriate physiological and anthropometric profile at the high-level." — [Mov Sport Sci 2020](https://www.mov-sport-sciences.org/10.1051/sm/2020011)
- Mendes D, Travassos B, Carmo JM, et al. *Talent Identification and Development in Male Futsal: A Systematic Review.* Int J Environ Res Public Health 2022;19(17):10648 (32 studies). Elite players show better body fat %, but "anthropometric characteristics and physical abilities do not necessarily make a difference between players in the post-pubertal period." — [Mendes et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9517923/)
- Female players: Gomez-Campos et al. 2023 (Eur J Transl Myol) reviewed 31 studies from 2010–2020 and found that elite players had higher weight, height and BMI than non-elite players. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/8a434c77d455454d8e1b4ebb8791d70e)

**Primary studies with a stated method (male unless noted)**
- **DXA, professional and semi-professional players, Portugal**: Giro R, Matias CN, Campa F, et al. Nutrients 2022;14(21):4514; DOI 10.3390/nu14214514. n = 78 (54 professional players in the top league, including national-team players; 24 semi-professional players in the 2nd and 3rd leagues, 3 sessions per week plus 1 match). Whole sample: body mass 72.8 kg (range 55.7–99.1), stature 176.0 cm, BMI 23.5 (19.1–29.5), **DXA FM 15.8% (range 10.5–25.6%; SD 3.2)**, FM 11.4 kg, FFM 60.0 kg. Body composition was not reported separately by level, and positions were not stratified. The DXA FM% test-retest error (TEM) was 1.7%. — [Giro et al. 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9655567/)
- **DXA, professional players, Brazil**: Gomes SA et al. J Phys Educ Sport 2022 (October, art. 325). n = 18 in the National Futsal League (50% wingers), mean age 21.1. Body mass 71.2 ± 7.3 kg (55.7–81.3), height 1.76 m, BMI 22.95 (19.8–26.2), **DXA BF 7.15 ± 2.87% (4.0–12.6%)**, lean body mass 62.6 kg. — [Gomes et al. 2022 (PDF)](https://www.efsupit.ro/images/stories/octombrie2022/Art%20325.pdf)
- **Contradiction**: the two DXA samples differ by about 8.7 percentage points in mean FM% (15.8% vs 7.15%). Device (Hologic vs Lunar), population and sample size all differ. This shows that even "gold-standard" figures are not universal norms.
- **Skinfolds (and derived %fat), U19 national team, Bosnia and Herzegovina**: Katanić B, et al. Sport Mont 2024; DOI 10.26773/smj.240212. n = 14. Body mass ranged from **69.7 kg (wingers)** to 93.7 kg (goalkeepers). Body fat: **wingers 5.9%**, defenders 9.3%, pivots 16.2%, goalkeepers 20.3%. BMI: wingers 21.5, defenders 21.7, pivots 25.7, goalkeepers 29.3. Team mean 11.63 ± 5.50% (4.1–20.4%). Juniors with a very small sample, and the authors urge caution. — [Katanić et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.26773/smj.240212)
- **BIA (InBody 270), three competitive levels, Portugal**: Belo J, Valente-dos-Santos J, et al. 2024. n = 84 (29 elite, 29 sub-elite, 26 non-elite). "no differences in relation to the body composition of the athletes" between levels. Jump power and functionality did discriminate between levels. — [Belo et al. 2024, PMC11125764](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11125764/) (journal name not verified in this session)
- **BIA, non-professional players, Italy**: Komici K, et al. 2023. n = 31 amateur players training 5 × 1.5 h per week plus a weekend game. BMI 23.69 ± 2.02, **FM 12.24 ± 3.61%**, FM 8.97 kg. — [Komici et al. 2023, PMC10583136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10583136/). This is one of the few datasets on **amateur** players, not split by position.
- **Professional players, Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina**: Sekulic D, et al. 2021. n = 75, height 182.3 ± 6.2 cm, body mass 80.8 ± 10.4 kg, a taller and heavier population. — [Sekulic et al. 2021, PMC7835319](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7835319/)
- **ISAK skinfolds, world-level players, preseason**: Chambel C, et al. n = 15. Body mass 77.0 (7.5) kg, sum of 8 skinfolds 67.8 (22.0) mm, muscle mass 34.3 kg (Lee equation). — [SPONET record](https://www.sponet.fi/Record/4087434)
- **Female players (context only)**: Brazilian elite players, n = 396 over 20 years, 3-skinfold Siri method. BF fell from 22.7% in 2001 to 19.0% in 2021. — [Weber et al. 2024, Eur J Transl Myol, DOI 10.4081/ejtm.2024.11887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11017169/). Spanish 1st-division players, n = 88, ISAK and a 4-component anthropometric model: pivots and goalkeepers were heaviest. — [Castillo et al. 2022, Appl Sci 12(11):5708](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/11/5708)

**Why the methods are not interchangeable**
- In Giro et al. 2022, none of 9 published skinfold equations accurately predicted DXA FM% in futsal players. Bias ranged from −8.2% to −1.3%. Lohman underestimated by 8.2%, Stewart by 5.8% and Evans 3SKF by 5.6%; Suarez-Arrones overestimated by 1.0%. R² ≤ 0.76, SEE ≥ 1.59%. The authors built a futsal-specific equation (R² = 0.85, SEE = 1.32%, agreement ±2.5%). — [Giro et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9655567/)
- In Gomes et al. 2022, the Jackson-Pollock 7-skinfold and 3-skinfold (subscapular-triceps-chest) equations significantly overestimated BF% compared with DXA. Only the 3-skinfold chest-abdomen-thigh equation agreed (within about 0.5 percentage points). — [Gomes et al. 2022](https://www.efsupit.ro/images/stories/octombrie2022/Art%20325.pdf)
- UEFA 2021: laboratory methods, DXA included, have "precision errors between 1% and 4.5%". Different DXA units "while not interchangeable" are precise. UEFA recommends **absolute skinfold sums rather than %fat equations** to track change, measured by ISAK-trained staff, and standardizing food intake and hydration for DXA. — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/bjsports/55/8/416.full.pdf)
- IOC 2023: "doubly indirect" methods (BIA-type, which rely on regression equations) "do not provide valid data, use spurious assumptions and/or are influenced greatly by athlete presentation (eg, hydration levels)". The proposed methods are skinfolds, DXA and B-mode ultrasound. — [Mountjoy et al. 2023](https://forening.sls.se/media/a4cl4crj/idrottsnutrition_2023-ioc-consenses-on-red-s-1.pdf)
- ACSM/ADA/DC 2000: careful skinfolds or BIA have a body-fat error of about 3–4% and an FFM error of about 2.5–3.5 kg. Example: a true 15% could read as 12–18%. — [ACSM 2000](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2000/12000/nutrition_and_athletic_performance.25.aspx)

### Inferences
- At 63 kg and 1.72 m, the user is lighter than most published winger means (about 68–70 kg). Those means mostly come from elite or junior-national players, often taller, measured with mixed methods. Being lighter than the mean does not mean being "wrong". UEFA says players can perform well outside the usual ranges, and the user's BMI (21.3) is almost identical to the U19 wingers' BMI of 21.5.
- A home BIA scale reading cannot be compared with DXA or skinfold reference values, so the app should not ask for or interpret %fat.

### Gaps
- I found almost no position-specific data for **adult amateur wingers**. Most position data are elite, junior or female.
- I found no recent (2023 or later) meta-analysis that pools male futsal body composition by position with method stratification.
- The original de Moura 2013 table was seen only through Spyrou's citation, which has unit inconsistencies.

## 3. Relationship between body composition and futsal performance

### Takeaway
The associations are consistent but modest and come from cross-sectional studies. Higher fat percentage goes with worse reactive strength, agility, sprint and aerobic capacity. More muscle mass goes with better jumping. No study shows that changing body weight improves futsal performance, and level differences are explained more by power and skill than by body composition.

### Cited Findings
- Sekulic et al. 2021 (n = 75 professional players): body fat % was negatively related to the reactive strength index (16% of shared variance) and to futsal-specific reactive agility with dribbling (10% of shared variance). The authors acknowledge the cross-sectional design. Top-level players were distinguished by reactive strength, broad jump, kicking speed and reactive agility, not by body composition. — [Sekulic et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7835319/)
- Chambel et al. (n = 15, world-level players): body mass and muscle mass correlated with countermovement jump (CMJ) peak propulsive force (r = 0.754 and 0.563). Muscle mass correlated with CMJ height (r = 0.556). — [SPONET](https://www.sponet.fi/Record/4087434)
- Trabelsi et al. 2014 (n = 26 elite Tunisian players, 69.3 kg, 1.77 m): %fat was negatively associated with VO2max (r = −0.41). Body mass was a determinant of CMJ height (r = −0.41). — [Trabelsi et al. 2014, Am J Sports Sci Med](https://doi.org/10.12691/ajssm-2-4-4)
- Komici et al. 2023 (n = 31 amateur players, BIA): fat mass was inversely related to peak VO2 (% of predicted) and handgrip. Muscle mass and FFM predicted spirometry results. — [Komici et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10583136/)
- Giro et al. 2022 introduction: greater fat mass in futsal players is associated with lower aerobic capacity, lower vertical jump and more fatigue (citing earlier work). — [Giro et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9655567/)
- Female elite players, Spain (n = 12, ISAK): agility correlated negatively with the fat component. Muscle mass correlated positively with aerobic capacity, agility, speed and Abalakov jump; the study also tested RSA. — [Castillo et al. 2022, Appl Sci 12(22):11492](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/22/11492)
- Belo et al. 2024: body composition was similar across levels, but jump power and functionality discriminated. — [Belo et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11125764/)
- Spyrou 2020 recommends focusing on "maximal speed, lower-body power and strength, aerobic capacity, and lean muscle mass." — [Spyrou et al. 2020, search summary](https://www.doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.569897)
- UEFA: "it is not known if a change in physique would result in better performance." — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/8/416)
- Football context: Ishida et al. 2021 (collegiate soccer, n = 23) found BF% related to slower 10 m (r = 0.44) and 20 m (r = 0.50) sprints and lean body mass related to CMJ height (r = 0.50). They warn that low lean mass may limit performance. — [Ishida et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7838787/)

### Inferences
- Evidence points to **training (power, reactive strength, change of direction) and enough lean mass** as the levers, not a lower scale number. For a lean, light winger, the literature gives no support for losing weight. If anything, gaining strength and muscle with training would be the performance-relevant direction.

### Gaps
- No intervention studies in futsal show that changing body composition causes better sprint, RSA or agility.
- RSA specifically has little male futsal body-composition data.

## 4. Low energy availability and REDs (IOC 2023): the threshold, its caveats, and the risks of chasing a low weight

### Takeaway
REDs is caused by problematic (prolonged and/or severe) low energy availability (LEA). The often-quoted 30 kcal/kg FFM/day cutoff comes from short laboratory studies in sedentary women. The IOC now says it is debated, that it should not be used as a definitive clinical threshold, and that males appear to have a lower and more variable threshold (about 9–25 kcal/kg FFM/day). Pursuing a low weight or an "athletic look" is a named pathway to LEA. This is a strong argument for the app never computing or targeting weight or energy availability.

### Cited Findings (all from Mountjoy M, et al. Br J Sports Med 2023;57(17):1073–1097, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994 — [PDF](https://forening.sls.se/media/a4cl4crj/idrottsnutrition_2023-ioc-consenses-on-red-s-1.pdf))
- Definition of energy availability: "EA = (EI − EEE) / FFM", expressed in kcal/kg FFM/day, i.e. "the dietary energy left over and available for optimum function of body systems after accounting for the energy expended from exercise."
- REDs is "a syndrome of impaired physiological and/or psychological functioning … caused by exposure to problematic (prolonged and/or severe) low energy availability." LEA runs on a continuum from "adaptable" (mild and transient) to "problematic". — also [search summary](https://appliedsportpsych.org/site/assets/files/1113/mountjoy_et_al_-_2023_reds_update.pdf)
- Historical zones (from studies in sedentary women): about 45 kcal/kg FFM/day or more for mass gain; about 45 for weight maintenance; 30–45 for body or fat mass loss; 30 or less for LEA with health effects. The 30 threshold "was based on elegant but short-term laboratory studies … in a small sample of sedentary females … intended as a guide, rather than a diagnostic end-point." There are "risks in setting a definitive clinical threshold of EA due to many moderating factors."
- Males: "While a universal cut-off of 30 kcal/kg FFM/day as a threshold of LEA leading to some REDs outcomes in females is debated, such a cut-off or range at which males experience REDs-related symptoms is even less understood, but appears to be lower (eg, ~9 to 25 kcal/kg FFM/day)." Only 20% of original studies from 2018 to 2022 included male athletes.
- Effects in males: problematic LEA affects the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis, metabolic hormones, immune function and bone health, and causes negative performance outcomes and "decreased lean body mass accrual". Two emerging male indicators are "low libido and decreased morning erections".
- Measuring EA in free-living athletes carries a high burden and errors in intake, exercise expenditure, FFM and resting metabolic rate. This is a reason a consumer app cannot estimate EA reliably.
- Body image: pressure for an "athletic look" can lead to "body dissatisfaction and LEA, and then to symptoms of REDs, DE behaviours or EDs". The athlete entourage often "lack[s] the knowledge of safe regulation of body weight". "Erroneous and intensive body composition measurement could lead to allegations of harassment and abuse."
- Safeguards: assessment should be done "by the athlete health and performance team who are trained in the specific methods", with "prescreening to assess body image concerns and problematic eating behaviours". Body composition data "are considered health data and must be kept confidential", and need informed consent. Under 18, body composition assessment is recommended "only for medical purposes".
- The IOC REDs Clinical Assessment Tool, version 2 (CAT2) is the clinical tool for professionals.
- Low carbohydrate availability is highlighted as a contributor, and REDs overlaps with overtraining syndrome. — [search summary of Mountjoy 2023](https://appliedsportpsych.org/site/assets/files/1113/mountjoy_et_al_-_2023_reds_update.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app should not compute EA. That would require FFM (which needs a valid body composition method), exercise energy expenditure and intake, all of which carry large errors. It should not present 30 kcal/kg FFM/day as a target or limit either.
- Warning signs could appear in the app's safety text as general information with a referral, not as diagnosis. For men: low libido, loss of morning erections, fatigue, poor performance, frequent injury or illness. The CLAUDE.md medical-advice limit applies here.
- A lean winger at 63 kg who restricts intake in a quest to be "lighter and faster" is exactly the scenario the IOC warns about.

### Gaps
- No futsal-specific REDs prevalence data were retrieved.
- The IOC statement's prevalence figures for team sports were not extracted in this session.

## 5. Healthy lean mass gain without restriction: surplus and protein

### Takeaway
For a player who wants to gain muscle, the evidence supports a **modest** energy surplus together with resistance training and adequate protein. Figures: about 1,500–2,000 kJ/day (about 360–480 kcal); 10–20% above maintenance for novices; gain of about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week; protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day spread over 3–6 meals. Evidence quality is moderate to low: narrative reviews, mostly in bodybuilders or resistance-trained people, not futsal players.

### Cited Findings
- Slater GJ, Dieter BP, Marsh DJ, Helms ER, Shaw G, Iraki J. *Is an Energy Surplus Required to Maximize Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy Associated With Resistance Training.* Front Nutr 2019;6:131; DOI 10.3389/fnut.2019.00131. "The specific energy surplus required to facilitate muscle hypertrophy is unknown." "Practitioners are advised to start conservatively with an energy surplus within the range of ~1,500–2,000 kJ·day⁻¹ and closely monitor response." Body composition and functional capacity should guide adjustments. There is no clear benefit to taking the surplus from a specific macronutrient, and "the health implications of sustained protein intakes above ~2.5 g·kg⁻¹·day⁻¹ remain to be validated." — [Slater et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2019.00131/full)
- Iraki J, Fitschen P, Espinar S, Helms E. *Nutrition Recommendations for Bodybuilders in the Off-Season: A Narrative Review.* Sports (Basel) 2019;7(7):154; DOI 10.3390/sports7070154. For novice and intermediate lifters: +10–20% above maintenance and a weekly gain of about 0.25–0.5% of body weight. For advanced lifters: +5–10% and about 0.25% per week. Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day, 0.40–0.55 g/kg per meal over 3–6 meals; fat 0.5–1.5 g/kg; carbohydrate at least 3–5 g/kg. In elite athletes, about 600 kcal more than ad libitum intake gave non-significantly more lean body mass (1.7 vs 1.2 kg) but significantly more fat (1.1 vs 0.2 kg), which led to the suggestion that 200–300 kcal/day may suit highly trained athletes. — [Iraki et al. 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/7/7/154); [Table 1, PMC](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6680710/table/sports-07-00154-t001/?report=objectonly)
- The 1.6–2.2 g/kg protein range rests on the Morton et al. 2018 meta-analysis (as cited by Iraki). — [Iraki et al. 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/7/7/154)
- A secondary source (Strength Cond J 2020, "Magnitude and Composition of the Energy Surplus…") suggests 250–500 kcal/day for trained people and 500–1,000 for novices, and says protein beyond about 1.6–2.2 g/kg "seems likely to be unnecessary". — [NSCA SCJ 2020](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-scj/fulltext/2020/10000/magnitude_and_composition_of_the_energy_surplus.9.aspx)
- UEFA 2021: increasing fat-free soft tissue mass "may be a desired training adaptation with benefits of enhanced strength and power". Interventions "should be justified, well planned out and executed" with the nutritionist and the performance team. — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/bjsports/55/8/416.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a 63 kg player, 0.25–0.5% per week means about 0.16–0.32 kg per week (arithmetic on the Iraki figures). Protein at 1.6–2.2 g/kg is about 100–139 g/day. These figures come from bodybuilding and resistance-training research, so the app should label them that way and mark them "pendiente de validar por un dietista-nutricionista" (pending validation by a dietitian-nutritionist), as the CLAUDE.md rule requires.
- In futsal, carbohydrate needs on training and match days also rise, so the "remaining calories from carbohydrate" approach fits the app's day-type engine.

### Gaps
- I found no studies of lean mass gain specifically in futsal players.
- The ACSM/AND/DC 2016 protein range for athletes (commonly cited as 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day) was not re-verified in this session. Check it in the full text before citing it in code.

## 6. What a non-professional app can responsibly do

### Takeaway
All three statements point the same way. Do not compute a target weight or a target %fat. If reference data are shown, show them as **ranges with explicit caveats** about level, method and population. Treat body data as sensitive health data. Refer the player to a sports dietitian-nutritionist (and an ISAK anthropometrist or sports scientist) for any assessment. The app's role is fuelling the training week, never restriction.

### Cited Findings
- Ranges rather than absolute goals; technical errors limit the use of measurements. — [Thomas et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/297695609_Nutrition_and_Athletic_Performance)
- "No single value for either BM or FM content against which targets or judgements should be made"; goals to be agreed with the performance team. — [Collins et al. 2021](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/8/416)
- Assessment only by trained teams, prescreening for body image concerns and disordered eating, "health first–performance second", confidential data with consent. Methods: skinfolds, DXA, ultrasound; "doubly indirect" methods are not valid. — [Mountjoy et al. 2023](https://forening.sls.se/media/a4cl4crj/idrottsnutrition_2023-ioc-consenses-on-red-s-1.pdf)
- BMI is not appropriate as a body composition surrogate in athletes. — [Dietitians of Canada summary of the 2016 paper](https://www.dietitians.ca/DietitiansOfCanada/media/Documents/Resources/noap-position-paper.pdf)
- The ACSM 2000 statement recommended that "daily weigh-ins are discouraged" (historical). — [ACSM 2000](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2000/12000/nutrition_and_athletic_performance.25.aspx)

### Inferences (design suggestions derived from the sources)
- Body weight stays in the form only as an **input for g/kg scaling** of fuelling (as it already is in v0.1). It is never an output or a goal, and there are no "ideal weight" or BMI verdicts.
- Answer "what is my optimal weight?" with a fixed, sourced explanation: there is no single ideal weight; performance, health and well-being matter more; and a dietitian-nutritionist with an ISAK or DXA assessment can help. Show futsal reference ranges only if they are clearly labelled with level, method and population, and with "not a target".
- Do not accept or interpret %fat from home BIA scales.
- Block weight-loss or "get leaner" requests at the rules layer, before the AI. Show a referral message instead. Consider a short safety note about REDs warning signs.
- The "gain muscle" path, if offered later, should use only modest surplus and protein figures with sources and the "pendiente de validar" (pending validation) flag, and always alongside resistance training and professional follow-up.

### Gaps
- I found no published guidance written specifically for consumer nutrition apps on presenting body-composition norms. The recommendations above are extrapolated from athlete-care statements.
